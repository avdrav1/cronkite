# Frame

Fast, lightweight Linux-native presentation application, easier to use than PowerPoint (user decision). Command `frame`. Design: [ADR 0004](../adr/0004-frame-presentation-model.md) (model and reading), [ADR 0006](../adr/0006-frame-editor.md) (editor).

## Format

`.pptx` only (user decision). `.ppt`, `.odp`, and `.key` are refused with a message naming the format ("Save it as .pptx first"). `.pptm` opens like `.pptx`; its macros never run.

## FR0: viewer

- `frame FILE` opens it; File → Open (Ctrl+O) opens a file dialog filtered to `.pptx`; dropping a file on the window opens it. Each file opens in its own window.
- Reading runs off the UI thread; the window shows "Opening…" until it is done. A file that can't be read gives an alert naming the file and the reason, and the window closes.
- A sidebar lists the slides as numbered thumbnails; the main area shows the selected slide scaled to fit, letterboxed. Page Down, Page Up, arrows, Home, and End move between slides; clicking a thumbnail selects it. Hidden slides show dimmed.
- Slides draw what PowerPoint shows for: slide size, backgrounds (solid), shapes with preset geometry (rectangles, rounded rectangles, ellipses, lines, and others drawn as their bounding rectangle), solid fills and outlines, rotation and flips, text with fonts, sizes, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, colours (including theme colours and their tints and shades), alignment, bullets and numbering with levels, vertical anchoring and insets, autofit's font scale, pictures (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP), tables (cell text, fills, borders), and groups. Placeholders take their position and text style from the slide's layout and master.
- What Frame can't draw yet (charts, SmartArt without a saved drawing, EMF/WMF/SVG pictures, video and embedded objects without a preview picture) draws as a labelled outline at its position, so nothing silently disappears. Gradients and patterns draw as one colour standing for them (a gradient's middle stop, a pattern's foreground); freeform and other preset shapes draw as their bounding rectangle.
- F5 starts a full-screen slideshow from the first slide, Shift+F5 from the current one; the slide fills the screen, letterboxed in black. Space, Enter, arrows, Page Up/Down, Backspace, and a click move; hidden slides are skipped. Escape, or going past the last slide, ends it, back in the window on the slide it showed.
- The window title is the file name; the status bar shows "Slide N of M".
- Accessibility: each slide's text is exposed as the canvas's accessible description.

## Easier than PowerPoint

Frame edits the same `.pptx` decks as PowerPoint with less to learn and fewer steps (user decision): direct editing on the slide, as in Keynote, plus an outline view for writing (user decision). The rules every FR1 feature follows:

- **No ribbon, one inspector.** The header bar holds New Slide, Insert (Text, Shape, Picture, Table), Outline, and Play. A sidebar inspector shows only what applies to the selection: with nothing selected, the slide (layout, background, hidden); with text, its font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour, alignment, bullets, and level; with a shape, its fill, outline, and geometry; with a table, its rows, columns, and cell fill. Changes apply as you make them; formatting never opens a dialog.
- **Things line up by themselves.** Moving or resizing a shape snaps its edges and centre to the slide's edges and centre, to the layout's placeholder positions, and to other shapes' edges and centres, within 6 screen pixels, drawing a guide line for each snap; three shapes in a row snap to equal spacing. Holding Alt turns snapping off. Arrow keys nudge the selection by one screen pixel, Shift+arrow by ten.
- **Layouts are pictures, and new slides guess right.** New Slide (Ctrl+M) adds a slide after the current one with its layout, or, after a title slide, the deck's Title and Content layout. The New Slide menu and the inspector's layout picker show the deck's own layouts as thumbnails. Changing a slide's layout moves its text into the new layout's matching placeholders.
- **Content goes where it belongs.** A picture dropped, pasted, or inserted fills the selected or first empty picture or content placeholder; with none, it lands centred, scaled to fit the slide. Text that overflows its placeholder shrinks to fit (PowerPoint's shrink on overflow, saved as `normAutofit`).
- **Writing in an outline.** View → Outline shows every slide's title and bullet text as one editable outline, as PowerPoint's outline view does: Enter after a title starts a new slide, Tab and Shift+Tab change a line's level (Tab on a title makes it a bullet of the slide before; Shift+Tab on a first-level bullet makes it a new slide's title). Slides added from the outline take the Title and Content layout. Text outside the title and body placeholders (text boxes, tables, SmartArt) isn't in the outline and is never changed by it; such a slide shows its title and how many other items it holds. The outline and the slide edit the same deck and share one undo history.
- **Starting is one step.** File → New (Ctrl+N), or `frame` with no file, opens Frame's built-in blank 16:9 deck with a title slide, ready to type; there is no template gallery.
- **Undo, not confirmation.** Every edit undoes and redoes; Frame doesn't ask "are you sure" for anything undo can reverse. The only dialogs are opening and saving files and alerts.

**Measured:** the reference task, a 4-slide deck (title slide "Quarterly review" with a subtitle; "Highlights" with three bullets, one at the second level; "Revenue" with a picture from a file; "Next steps" with two bullets; saved as `.pptx`), takes fewer input actions in Frame than in PowerPoint. Input actions are clicks, drags, and key presses other than the content's own characters. Frame's count comes from its check script; PowerPoint's (Microsoft 365, fastest documented path) is derived step by step from Microsoft's documentation and recorded in the story's Notes. FRAME-15: Frame 15 on the canvas and 19 in the outline, PowerPoint 21.

## FR1: editor

Editing slide text on the slide and in the outline view, moving and resizing shapes with snapping, formatting through the inspector, inserting text boxes, shapes, pictures, and tables, adding, deleting, duplicating, and reordering slides, new decks from the built-in blank deck, and save by patching the original package, all with undo. Speaker notes come later.

Built:

- **Slide text.** A click in a text box or placeholder puts the caret where it lands (Shift+click selects to it); the canvas outlines empty placeholders, dashed, so there's somewhere to click. Typing, Backspace, Delete, Enter (a new paragraph with the same level, alignment, and bullet; numbers renumber), Shift+Enter (a line break), the arrows, Home and End (with Ctrl, the whole text's), Shift with any of them to select, Ctrl+A, and Escape to stop. Typed text takes the look of the character before the caret, or of an empty paragraph's mark, so a placeholder keeps its layout's style. While text is edited the arrows move the caret; Page Up and Page Down stop editing and change slides. As in PowerPoint, Ctrl+Enter puts the caret at the end of the slide's next title, subtitle, or body placeholder (with nothing edited, the first), and after the last one adds a slide as New Slide does; Tab and Shift+Tab at the start of a paragraph, or over several, move the paragraphs a level down or up with that level's looks, and elsewhere do nothing.
- **Slides.** New Slide (Ctrl+M, or the header bar's New Slide button) adds a slide after the current one on its layout, or on Title and Content after a title slide; the menu beside the button shows the deck's layouts as thumbnails of the slides they make, with their names, and picking one adds a slide on it. A new slide has its layout's placeholders, empty and outlined, with the caret in its title, ready to type. Ctrl+D duplicates the current slide; Delete or Backspace in the slide list deletes it; Ctrl+Up and Ctrl+Down, or dragging a thumbnail, move it. The slide list's context menu has New Slide, Duplicate Slide, and Delete Slide. The canvas's accessible name says the slide's layout ("Slide 2, Title and Content").
- **Shapes.** A click on a shape without text selects it: its box outlined in blue with eight handles; a click on the slide's empty space lets go, as does Escape; typing into a selected shape with a text body adds to its text. Dragging a shape moves it, dragging a handle of the selected one resizes it, and the shape whose text is edited moves by its border. While dragged, a box snaps, within 6 screen pixels, to the slide's edges and centre, the layout's placeholders, the other shapes' edges and centres, and equal spacing in a row or column, with magenta guides drawn for each; Alt drags freely. The arrows nudge the selected shape a screen pixel, Shift+arrows ten. A save writes a moved or resized shape's new offset and size into its `xfrm` (a placeholder that took its box from its layout gets one). Shapes inside groups, and SmartArt drawings' shapes, don't move; turned shapes move but don't resize.
- **Inspector.** A sidebar right of the canvas shows what applies to the selection. With nothing selected, the slide: its Layout (the deck's layouts, each with a thumbnail; picking one moves the slide's text into the new layout's matching placeholders, which give it their looks; the looks set on the text stay) and Background (a colour). With text being edited, or a shape with text selected: Font (a searchable list of the system's families), Size, Bold, Italic, Underline, Colour, alignment, and Bullets; text looks apply to the selected text, or the whole text when none is selected, paragraph looks to the paragraphs the selection touches. With a shape selected: its Fill and Outline (colour and width) and its geometry; a picture shows its format and pixel size, a table its rows and columns. A value the shape doesn't set itself is captioned with where it comes from ("From the layout"). Colours come from a palette of swatches in a popover; nothing opens a dialog. Each change applies at once as one undo step; a save writes it on the shape (the run's or paragraph's properties, the shape's fill and line, the slide's background, the slide's layout relationship and placeholders).
- **Inserting.** The header bar's Insert menu adds, centred on the slide: Text (a text box with "Text" selected, so typing replaces it; it grows to fit its text), a Shape (rectangle, rounded rectangle, oval, triangle, or line, in the accent colour, ready to type into), a Picture (from the file dialog), or a Table (3 by 3, a coloured header row and banded rows). A picture inserted, dropped on the canvas, or pasted (Ctrl+V) fills the selected or first empty picture or content placeholder, cropped to its shape; with none, it lands centred at its own size, scaled down to fit the slide. Insert works while the outline is shown too, on the slide the outline's caret is on, and the keys go back to the outline. Delete or Backspace deletes the selected shape. Text that overflows its placeholder shrinks to fit, in PowerPoint's steps (saved as `normAutofit`), and Ctrl+Z takes back the typing and the shrink together.
- **Outline.** The header bar's Outline button (Ctrl+Shift+O) shows the deck as one editable outline in place of the canvas: each slide's number and title, its body's paragraphs as bullets indented by level, and, for a slide with anything else, how many other items it holds (not editable, never changed). Enter after a title starts a Title and Content slide with the rest of the title; Enter in a bullet starts the next bullet; Ctrl+Enter anywhere starts an empty Title and Content slide after the caret's. Tab moves a bullet a level down, and on a title makes the slide a bullet of the slide before, its bullets a level down with it (only for a slide holding nothing else); Shift+Tab moves a bullet a level up, and a first-level bullet becomes the title of a new slide with the bullets after it. Backspace at the start of a bullet joins it to the one before, and at the start of an empty slide's title deletes the slide. Moving through the outline shows the slide the cursor is on in the slide list. Outline edits are the canvas's edits: one undo history, one save.
- **New.** `frame` without a file, and Ctrl+N, open Frame's built-in blank deck in a window titled Untitled: 16:9 (13.333 by 7.5 inches), the Office theme, an empty title slide with the caret in its title, and five layouts (Title Slide, Title and Content, Section Header, Two Content, Title Only). Saving an untitled deck asks where (Save As); then the window is that file's.
- **Undo.** Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes, a word of typing, one slide change, one move, resize, or nudge, one inspector change, one insert or delete, or one outline edit at a time, going to the slide the step changed.
- **Saving.** Ctrl+S (the header bar's Save button) saves over the deck's file; Ctrl+Shift+S saves it as another file through the file dialog, and the window becomes that file's. A save patches the package the deck was read from (ADR 0006): parts no edit touches are copied as they were; an edited shape's paragraphs are written back with the markup they were read with; new slides get the part PowerPoint writes for a slide on their layout; a duplicate gets a copy of its slide's part, relationships, and notes; deleted slides' parts and notes go; the slide list, its sections, relationships, and content types follow. Saving is atomic, runs off the UI thread while the window waits, and reads the written file back before it replaces the old one; a failed save leaves the file as it was and says why.
- **Keys on macOS.** On macOS, Command replaces Ctrl in every shortcut (MAC-3, `apps/frame/src/keys.rs`); Redo is ⇧⌘Z only (Ctrl+Y is Linux's), and New Slide is ⇧⌘N, as in PowerPoint for Mac, since ⌘M minimizes. In slide text, ⌥←/⌥→ move a word, ⌘←/⌘→ to the line's start and end, ⌘↑/⌘↓ to the text's, Shift extending the selection; ⌥⌫ and ⌥⌦ delete a word, ⌘⌫ to the line's start.
- **Menus on macOS.** The menu bar has GTK's app menu (About Frame, Update Automatically, Settings, Services, Hide, Quit ⌘Q), then File (New, Open, Close Window ⌘W, Save, Save As), Edit (Undo, Redo), Insert (Text, Shape, Picture, Table), Slide (New, Duplicate, Delete, Move Up, Move Down), View (Outline, Slideshow, Slideshow from This Slide), and Window (Minimize ⌘M, Zoom, the windows), each item with its key (MAC-4). Windows keep macOS's title bar; New Slide with its layouts, Insert, Outline, and the slideshow button are a toolbar row over the deck, as New, Open, and Save are in the File menu. Linux keeps the header bar.
- **Install.** A per-user curl installer, as Brick has (`sites/frame/install.sh`: `curl -fsSL https://frame.cronkite.cc/install.sh | sh`, `| sh -s -- --uninstall`), with no sudo. It checks the release against its sha256. It puts `frame` in `~/.local/bin` and its menu entry and icon (`dev.cronkite.Frame`, the app ID) in `~/.local/share`, and makes Frame the default for `.pptx`, so double-clicking a presentation opens it. Uninstalling removes them and Frame's `.pptx` default; other apps' defaults stay. GTK 4 comes from the system; without it, the installer says how to get it.
- **Install on macOS (MAC-7).** Frame is `Frame.app` for Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later, carrying GTK and everything it reads (no Homebrew), signed ad hoc. The same curl command puts it in `~/Applications` and registers it with Launch Services, so `.pptx` and `.pptm` presentations list it under Open With; it doesn't change the Mac's default apps (Finder's Get Info, Open with, Change All does), and `--uninstall` removes it and its registration. The download page also offers a DMG, with Gatekeeper's Open Anyway steps.
- **Fonts and appearance on macOS (MAC-6).** Frame carries Liberation, Carlito, and Caladea and draws through fontconfig as on Linux, so a deck's Calibri, Cambria, Arial, Times New Roman, and Courier New text breaks lines as on Linux with nothing to install. The window follows the Mac's light or dark appearance, also when it changes while Frame runs; slides keep their own colours.
- **Opening from Finder on macOS (MAC-5).** Finder's Open and Open With, a deck dropped on Frame's Dock icon, and `open -a Frame` open each deck in a window of its own in the running Frame (Launch Services keeps one Frame, and GTK hands it the files); a deck already open brings its window forward. Started without a file it opens a new deck, and started for a deck only that deck's window, no Untitled one beside it. On Linux each `frame FILE` is its own process, as before. Frame draws with cairo on macOS unless `GSK_RENDERER` names another renderer: GTK's GL renderer drew black windows on GitHub's macOS runners and is untried on a real Mac.
- **Fonts and opening on Windows (WIN-4).** Frame carries Liberation, Carlito, and Caladea and draws through fontconfig, over Windows' own fonts (Calibri, Cambria, Arial, and the rest draw as themselves; decks naming the stand-ins draw in the carried ones), laying slide text out with the fonts' own widths as on Linux. A deck on the command line, from Explorer's Open with, or by a registered type opens in a Frame process of its own, as on Linux; a deck dragged from Explorer onto a window opens. GTK chooses the renderer (cairo without a GPU).
- **Windows on Windows (WIN-3).** Frame is a GUI program, so no console window opens with it; `frame --version` still prints in the terminal it was started from (after the next prompt) or into a file or pipe. Windows have Windows' own title bar (minimize, maximize, close, snapping), and under it a menu bar row with the macOS menus (File, Edit, Insert, Slide, View) and Help (Update Automatically, About Frame); File ends with Exit. New Slide with its layouts, Insert, Outline, and the slideshow button are the toolbar row over the deck, as on macOS. Shortcuts stay Control, as on Linux. Frame follows Windows' app mode, light or dark, the title bar too, also when it changes while Frame runs; slides keep their own colours.
- **Installing on Windows (WIN-5).** Frame installs for the user alone, without admin rights, from its Setup.exe or `irm https://frame.cronkite.cc/install.ps1 | iex`, into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Frame` with GTK and the fonts inside and a Start menu entry. PowerPoint presentations (`.pptx .pptm`) list it under Open with and in Settings → Default apps; Windows keeps the user's defaults. Settings → Apps uninstalls it; what Frame keeps in `%APPDATA%\Cronkite\Frame` and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Cronkite\Frame` stays (uninstalling by hand asks whether to remove it too).
- **Download page on Windows (WIN-6).** frame.cronkite.cc offers a Windows visitor the Setup.exe on its button, the PowerShell command first under Install, and, for a Setup.exe downloaded in a browser, SmartScreen's way past "Windows protected your PC" (More info → Run anyway); Linux and Mac visitors keep theirs first, and every platform's download stays listed.
- **Updates (UPD-3).** Frame updates itself (ADR 0012): an install from the installer checks its release folder once each start, off the UI thread, downloads a newer release in the background when its Ed25519 signature and sha256 verify, and runs it from the next start (UPD-2 to UPD-6). "Update Automatically" turns it off (UPD-3): a check item in the ☰ main menu at the header bar's end (with About Frame) on Linux, after About Frame in the Frame menu on macOS, and in Help on Windows; on by default, kept in the settings folder's `update` file. `CRONKITE_NO_UPDATE=1` turns it off for one run; off, Frame makes no network request. The first start of a version an update applied says "Updated to Frame X.Y.Z" once, in a banner over the top of its first window that never takes the keys and goes when dismissed or after 20 seconds.

## Non-goals for now

Animations and transitions (kept in the file, not played), audio and video playback, presenter view, collaboration.
